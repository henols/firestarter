## Requirement completion

**RIG-05** — this plan closes RIG-05. Plan 04 built the script gate that proves a record is
complete and plan 07 pinned the substrate; this plan supplies the reconstruction that proves the
record is *sufficient*. Neither alone suffices: a script-only gate proves the record has all its
fields, not that a party holding only that record could rebuild the run; a reconstruction-only
proof leaves later cells unchecked, so a field that quietly stops being captured mid-sweep would
go unnoticed until the close. With both in place RIG-05 is complete, and with plans 02 and 10
closing RIG-01, plan 06 closing RIG-03, plan 11 closing RIG-02 and plan 12 closing RIG-04, all
five phase requirements are then closed. Update the requirement checkboxes only after this plan

  the run genuinely needed. This is a finding about the record, not about the reconstructing
  context, and it is the outcome the exercise exists to surface. Fix it at the source: add the
  field to `capture_provenance.py`'s gathered set, re-capture the record for this position, and
  **re-run the reconstruction with a fresh context** against the corrected record. Record both
  rounds. Do not edit the diff to agree and do not patch the record by hand.
- **Prescription ambiguity** — the procedure was readable in more than one way and the
  reconstruction took a different reading. Fix the procedure's wording, re-render the step
  lists, confirm the arm-agnostic diff is still empty, and record the amendment.

The pass condition is that after any such fix, the reconstruction's command set and physical
setup are equivalent to the prescription with **zero** values sourced from anywhere but the two
inputs. State that conclusion in one explicit sentence, with the count of divergences of each
kind and the count of rounds it took.

Two things to record honestly rather than smooth over. If the reconstruction produced a command
whose first token was a bare application name rather than an absolute arm binary path, that is a
record insufficiency of the most consequential kind — the record failed to make the arm
unambiguous — and it must be fixed and re-run, not explained. And if the reconstruction could
not determine which shield revision to ask the operator for, note that the record carries the
declared value and the reconstruction should have found it; if it did not, the field's placement
or naming in the record is the defect.
