**The write op key on both is `write-partial`, not `write`.** Every per-step lookup must accept
both — a lookup on the plain name alone reads these two positions' write cells as empty, which is
Pitfall 3 in the research and the single most likely silent defect in this plan.

Both parts' write regions are a **single 256-byte slot** selected top-down from the device's current
contents, with the payload derived as a mask over the existing bytes and guaranteed to clear at
least 64 bits and retain at least 64 bits of the slot's 2048. That is a far gentler electrical
exercise than a full-device programming pass, and it is why the historical baselines — which used a
dense ramp into virgin space — are not comparable.

## The one pot move, and why the target is not the nominal one

The database target for both parts is **13000 mV**. The firmware's own guard is **asymmetric**: a
reading above the target plus 500 mV raises a **blocking** error whose only downgrade is a
permanently withdrawn flag, while a reading below 95% of target raises a **non-blocking warning**.
Phase 161's cell A3/B2 measured this rig's firmware voltage reading against a meter and found the
firmware reads roughly **7.5% high** (measured range 6.8% to 8.3%, from three paired readings — see
`bench/cells/A3-B2/POT.md`).

So a real rail at the nominal 13.0 V would read around 14 V to the firmware and **hard-block the
run**. D-12's policy is therefore: **set the highest real rail whose firmware reading stays under
13500 mV.** In practice that means aiming for a firmware reading around **13.2–13.3 V**, which at
the measured ratio is a real rail of roughly **12.3–12.4 V** — about **0.9 V more real rail** than
the sweep has been running on, and it lands on the two parts whose writes are irrecoverable.

Three things follow, all of which go in `POT.md`:

- **Chasing the target upward is the only thing on this rig that can block a run.** Running under
  target cannot. If in doubt, go lower.
- **Every figure recorded here is idle.** The firmware reading is an idle analogue sample and the
  meter reading is an operator measurement at rest. Neither is a program-window measurement, and
  v1.34 makes **no electrical claim**. State that on the record.
- **A blank or `0x303` reading is a contact fault**, because the voltage monitors do not route to
  the socket. That is `P-H1` — a halt and a re-seat, not a pot adjustment.

Per PD-17 this is the **second and last** meter reading of the sweep. Position 9 carries the
operator's figure in `vpp_real_mv`; position 10 records the named-absence shape pointing back at
position 9 with the group ratio.

  `.planning/v1.15/bench/EVIDENCE.md:61` (**PASS**, N=3, not blank); v1.15 Phase 83 write attempt,
  `:167` and the anomaly handoff at `:179`; v1.15 Phase 84 Task 3a re-bench, `:222`–`:237`
  (**DEFERRED**, the zero-bits disposition v1.18 later refuted); v1.18 Phases 97–99, whose scoped
  32-pin pin map with a dedicated read-write pin is **live on the v1.33 arm** and is why v1.15's
  disposition is superseded rather than compared against; and the ledger's open-defect entry.
- **Verdict, booked in advance (PD-5):** **`diverges: no comparable baseline`**, on four independent
  grounds, any one sufficient. The prior figures are a **64-byte** dense ramp written into two
  mid-device all-ones regions, against this command's **256-byte** masked slot near the top of the
  device — a different **region**, a different **length**, and materially different **pattern
  semantics**, since a ramp into virgin space drives roughly half of all bits to zero while the mask
  guarantees only 64 of 2048. The prior writes used a **now-forbidden flag** — the same ground D-03
  already accepts for the other family — and PD-6's shared clause applies: that flag's era semantics
  also skipped the erase. And the prior **disposition itself is not a per-step verdict**: a deferral
  is a milestone-level judgement about a fix, outside the vocabulary D-02 can flip against.
- **Where a comparable baseline *does* exist, and must still be used.** The write path has none, but
  three steps do, all from v1.18's own pre-work record at `.planning/v1.18/bench/EVIDENCE.json:20`:
  `id` — the chip identity was confirmed; `read` — a three-run oracle was byte-identical and a
  post-write three-run consistency check gave one distinct digest; and `blank-check` — the part was
  recorded **not blank** with data at the device's first address. A flip in any of those three **is**
  a genuine finding and is named inside the same verdict string after the no-comparable-baseline
  clause.
- **`erase` is structurally NA** — an ultraviolet part cannot be erased electrically. Record it with
  its verbatim reason and never as a flip.
- **The control re-run is guaranteed non-arbitrating, and the row must say so.** Slot selection is
  **stateless and keyed on the chip's current contents**, so once the v1.33 run has written the top
  slot the control run lands on the **next slot down** — a different region of a part the record
  already calls non-deterministic. A v1.33-versus-control difference on this part therefore cannot
  distinguish arm from silicon. D-05 also classifies this part as non-deterministic, so symptom
  variance goes in `anomalies` and never triggers a verdict change.
- **`known_carried`:** **yes** — SC#5. The text carries the verbatim marginality figures, the
  citation to the v1.18 cell, the ledger's open-defect entry, and the explicit statement that the
  record calls this part unable to arbitrate anything.
- **The continuity read (PD-8).** Before the `dev test` run, take **one**
  `dev consistency-check AM27C020 --runs 1` — non-destructive, roughly forty-five seconds — and
  compare the full-device digest against the v1.18 post-write digest recorded in
  `.planning/v1.18/bench/EVIDENCE.json`. Nothing between v1.19 and v1.33 touched this part at the
  bench, so the digests should match. A mismatch means either an undocumented touch or a read fault,
  and either changes how the row is read — record the outcome in `anomalies` **either way**.
