<objective>
Close the two tests that have been red since plan `197-02`, by measuring what actually moved on the
wire rather than assuming it.

Research measured the symptom — `assert len(changed_keys) == 84` finding 87 — and attributed the
three extra records to the Fujitsu pulse rows. That attribution is probably right and it is not
proven, because Python evaluates asserts in order: the length assertion fails first, so the
`changed_fields == {"flags"}` assertion on the next lines never ran, and nobody has observed its
post-change value. Meanwhile D-17 moves `RAMTRON/FM1608`'s `electrical.vcc_mv` from 3300 to 5000. If
that surfaces on the wire it is a fourth delta record and a second changed field, and a plan that
hardcodes "three records, field `pulse-delay`" would then be writing a golden that does not match
reality and asserting an exact count against it.

So this plan measures first and writes second. The layer is the complete residual of the live capture
against the composed golden, whatever that turns out to contain, and the exact-count assertion states
the measured number.

The composition choice is made: option (a). The new layer goes into the `expected` composition of
`test_exactly_84_records_change_flags_and_no_other_field_moves` alongside 149, 182 and 194. That keeps
the count at 84 and `changed_fields` at exactly `{"flags"}` — which is the assertion that catches a
second, unnoticed wire change riding along with an intended one. Bumping to 87 and widening the field
set to two entries would be the arithmetically obvious move and it would blunt precisely the guarantee
this milestone exists to enforce.

Purpose: record this phase's wire movement as a reviewable, exactly-counted, provably-non-vacuous
layer, and take the suite green.
Output: a new golden delta layer, a wired-in `tests/test_wire_dict_equivalence.py`, and a full
Python 3.11 suite with zero failures.
</objective>
