<objective>
Delete the two hardcodes that turned out to be defects rather than corrections, and record the
evidence the deferred half of one of them needs.

Both were part-number name lists standing in for a fleet-wide property — the same defect shape as
the community reports this milestone answers. Neither moves into the override file: D-05 is explicit
that only decoded values may be overridden and that everything else gets a real resolution.

The AT28C list is measured to achieve nothing beyond a reason-string swap. Its nine names are exactly
the nine rows the hardware-damage guard above it already selects with `flags & 0x10`, with zero
exceptions across all 19 `DIP24_2816` rows. The operator ruled during planning: delete the name list
now, route the guard narrowing to Phase 199. So zero rows flip to `supported` here, and the evidence
Phase 199 needs is filed rather than acted on.

The FM1608 relabel is measured to change two fields, not one. `electrical.type` goes `FRAM` to `SRAM`,
and `electrical.vcc_mv` goes 3300 to 5000 because the FRAM label was bypassing the SRAM single-rail
rewrite. The second field is not a display string, and it makes FM1608 consistent with its four
Ramtron siblings which already read `SRAM`.

**Known-red window.** The two `tests/test_wire_dict_equivalence.py` tests stay red; plan `197-06`
closes them. Every other test that this plan reddens is fixed inside this plan.

Purpose: get the remaining two part-specific hardcodes out of the generator without smuggling either
into the override file, and without touching a safety guard the operator has not ruled on.
Output: a `build_db.py` with no part-number literal left, two corrected test legs, a re-recorded
snapshot, and a Phase 199 evidence note.
</objective>
