<objective>
Take one voltage from a datasheet on disk all the way to the operator's screen, and close the VPP
half of the decode at the same time.

This is the phase's tracer. `FUJITSU/MBM27C4001` is the part gh#66 reported. Its own datasheet
specifies 12.5 V ± 0.3 V; the database asks for 12.0 V. This plan moves that one number and every
layer it passes through — the override file, the decoded input, the generated database, the wire
dictionary the firmware receives, the rendered `12.5v` an operator reads, and the test fixtures that
pin all four. Two sibling rows ride the same evidence: `MBM27C1001` takes the same VPP correction,
and all three Fujitsu rows whose vendored PDFs state 6.0 V ± 0.25 V take the `vdd_mv` correction
D-09 assigns to this phase rather than Phase 200.

The plan then completes `VPP_MV` from upstream's `xg_vpp_voltages[]` (D-02) and fixes the mask that
would collapse 25 V and 21 V onto 18 V. That change ships zero diff — no filtered row carries those
two indices today — so byte-identical regeneration is its proof.

**Edge-probe accounting for this plan: 2 of the phase's 7 probe-surfaced items.** VOLT-02/boundary
and VOLT-02/precision are both resolved `explicit` and authored as plain `must_haves.truths`
strings above. The remaining five are carried by plans `198-02` (3), `198-03` (1) and `198-04` (1).

**Known-red window, by design.** Task 1 reddens two tests in `tests/test_wire_dict_equivalence.py`
and Task 2 closes them by name. This mirrors 197-02 → 197-06 exactly. Do not attempt to keep the
suite green across Task 1; attempting it would mean re-baselining the golden, which is the one
outcome the delta-layer discipline exists to prevent.

Purpose: prove the whole stack end-to-end on one real correction before widening the decode, and
satisfy VOLT-02 on the row the community reported.
Output: three corrected Fujitsu rows, a completed and correctly-masked VPP decode, a two-entry wire
delta layer that has been seen to fail, and a two-line snapshot re-record.
</objective>
