site that asserts the pin-1 control bit; and recorded the whole chain in

The claim being tested: with `CTRL_VPP_REGULATOR_ENABLE` clear, the `VPE` rail sits near VCC rather
than at the programming voltage. It is inferred from the topology — `U1` is a MIC2288 boost converter
and a disabled boost passes VIN through its inductor and the `D1` Schottky to `VPE` — and no
committed artefact states it. It is what makes "reading is safe" true rather than merely "reading
asserts a bit whose voltage we did not check".

Why it matters: if the rail is near VCC with the regulator off, a `read` on an affected part drives
socket pin 1 with a logic level and the gate correctly covers `write` and `erase` only. If the rail is
boosted with the regulator off, then `read`, `verify` and `blank` all drive socket pin 1 from a
programming rail, they join the damage-capable set, and the gate's scope must widen.

The ONE thing requiring you:

1. Confirm which board this is, on sight. `hw_revision` cannot distinguish a Rev 2.2 from a Rev 2.0
   from a modified Rev 0, so state the board's identity rather than reading it.
2. Put the board in a state where the boost regulator is off. A board that is powered but idle, with
   no operation running, is the simplest — the regulator is enabled only inside an operation.
3. Take ONE reading: DC volts at `J6` pin 4, the `VPE` header pin, referenced to board GND.
4. Report the value, the reference point you used, and the board identity.

Optionally, if convenient in the same session: one further single reading at socket pin 1 in the same
idle state, so the record carries both the rail and the pin.

Do not hold a probe on a live rail while an operation runs. There is no live monitor and none is
wanted: the target is stated, you take the reading, you report it once.

Expected if the assumption holds: approximately VCC, in the region of 5 V. A reading near the
programming voltage, in the region of 12 V, falsifies it and widens the gate's scope.
  </instructions>
