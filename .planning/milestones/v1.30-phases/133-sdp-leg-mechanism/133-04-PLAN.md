<objective>
Make `run_plan` incapable of stranding a locked chip: a generic cleanup registry drained in one
`try/finally` around the whole step loop, with per-callable narrow error handling, and with an explicit
prohibition on the drain touching the list `run_plan` returns.

Purpose: this is the phase's durable value. Nothing here is user-visible; everything here is what stops
Phase 134's leg from leaving a locked part on a stranger's bench. Research P-20 is the hazard in its
original form — a step that never runs because the loop unwound leaves the part locked. LEG-10 forbids
it, and criterion 1 names the mechanism precisely: a `finally` reaches `KeyboardInterrupt`/`SystemExit`
"which `atexit` would not".

The plan's highest-severity trap is not the `finally` — it is what the drain records. The natural
implementation, which P-20's own prevention text describes, is `results.append(unlock_result)` in the
`finally`. Measured: that IS visible to the caller (same list reference), and that list feeds seven
consumers at `cli_handlers.py` — `count_applicable` would render "8 of 7 ran", plus the generic
renderer, the JSON artifact, the markdown table, `build_db_diff`, `dedup_fingerprint`, and
`sys.exit(max(...))`. In Phase 133 the registry is empty on every shipping run, so all seven effects
are zero — which is exactly what makes it dangerous. It would pass every test in this phase and
detonate in Phase 134.

Output: the registry, the `try/finally`, the registration site, the per-callable wrapper, LEG-10's five
proofs and LEG-09's two criterion-3 proofs.
</objective>

the resolver, the dispatch arm, and plan 133-02's exception mapping. Register only on OK — registering
after a failed lock would attempt to unlock a part that was never locked.

**The per-callable wrapper (D-10).** Inside the `finally`, iterate the registry in order and wrap each

parity reasoning can point at. On a caught exception, `continue` the drain; never re-raise from the
`finally`.

intended: an exception raised inside a `finally` replaces the in-flight exception, so a run-fatal
condition surfacing during cleanup must not be allowed to mask the original fault or the user's Ctrl-C.

oversight.
