<objective>
Document `protect_on_after` and its sibling `protect_off_before` once, in the artifact whose
declared job is "what this field means and what we do with it" — carrying the measurement
rather than a shrug, and stating plainly that nothing consumes them.

Purpose: DATA-06, D-13, D-14, D-15, D-16. DATA-06's fork was **closed by the Phase 150
deferral, not by a fresh choice**: the RELOCK-consumer branch became unreachable when
`write --sdp-relock` returned to Backlog 999.28, so the advisory branch is the only reachable
one and Phase 151 is now the only phase that may write about this field. The field stops being
dead data by being explained, and the explanation must not imply it is honoured.

Three constraints shape the wording. First, `MP_PROTECT_AFTER` means "can re-protect after
write" — it gates minipro `-P`, so it is a **capability, not a policy**. Second, its only
discriminating information anywhere is the `0x0D` ALLOW/REFUSE split, which `sdp_capability`
already transcribes and which
`tests/test_sdp_db_invariant.py::test_sdp_partition_matches_infoic_derived_field_element_wise`
already proves element-wise equal. Third, the by-algorithm headline figure is a trap: on
`algorithm: 13`, 66 of the 84 rows are **promoted from a foreign upstream protocol**, so
"43 of 84" is 18 of 18 upstream-native plus 25 of 66 promoted, and a sentence citing only the
43 is true of the database and misleading about the silicon.

Output: one authoritative section, two one-line pointers, a Python proof, and a folded todo
marked resolved.

**Requirement flips owned by this plan: DATA-06.** This is the only plan in the phase that
flips it.
</objective>
