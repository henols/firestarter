<objective>
Make the shield say what it cannot do instead of offering it, and route the only rail that might
reach the part.

`dim20` proposed this in gh#71: the standard VPP rail tops out near 18 V, the VPE rail has headroom,
so route VPE as VPP. The finding of this phase is that someone already built every part of that
mechanism — the flag, the CLI option, the firmware's route resolver, and an acceptance window that
already follows whichever rail is routed — and only an operator who knew the flag's name could reach
it. This plan adds the decision rule in front of it, and the warning RAIL-03 asks for.

The rule is two numbers the host already has: the part's required `vpp_mv` from the wire dict, and
the deliverable maximum of the drop-resistor path measured at socket pin 1 by plan `199-02`. That
second number does not exist until the bench session runs, which is why this plan depends on
`199-02` and why its threshold constant is filled by parsing the committed bench record rather than
by writing a figure into this plan. D-07 says the measurement beats the provisional 18 V outright;
a constant authored against 18000 here would have made that unobservable.

**Edge-probe accounting for this plan: 3 of the phase's 8 probe-surfaced items.** RAIL-01 boundary,
RAIL-03 empty and RAIL-03 encoding are all resolved explicit and authored as plain
`must_haves.truths` strings above. The remaining five are carried by plans `199-02` (2), `199-04` (1)
and `199-05` (2).

Purpose: satisfy RAIL-03 and RAIL-04, on the path the operator actually uses, without refusing
anything and without advising anything the tool cannot back.
Output: one pure policy module in the three-gate shape, two call sites, and a test module that proves
the warning is emitted, the flag is raised, and the operation still runs.
</objective>
