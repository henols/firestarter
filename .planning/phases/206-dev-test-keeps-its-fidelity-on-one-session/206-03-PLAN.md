<objective>
Let a `dev test` plan hold one validated link across its thirty-odd operator calls, behind one
default-off seam that one commit can take back.

Purpose: `EpromOperator` holds at most one `SerialCommunicator` at a time and tears it down in
`_operation_context`'s `finally` after every single call. A `dev test` plan for an erasable full-device
part at three cycles makes roughly thirty of those calls, and each one pays an unconditional two-second
stabilisation sleep inside `SerialCommunicator.__init__` on top of the port walk. The firmware imposes
no obstacle: `command_done()` sets `handle->cmd = CMD_IDLE` and `loop()` reads the next framed command
off the same open link. The blocker is entirely host-side, and it is that `find_and_connect` fuses the
port walk with sending the operation's own setup command and validating the firmware and hardware gates
off the resulting ack — so the send-and-validate half has to be extractable before it can run a second
time on an already-open port.

The second purpose is structural. SESS-02 may require this feature to be removed, and "kept on
principle" is exactly what the requirement forbids. So the shape is fixed before the measurement: one
permanent, behaviour-identical extraction commit that is kept either way, and one default-off feature
commit with a single caller that `git revert` takes back whole.

Output: `EpromOperator.lease()`, off by default, on at one call site, with its firmware and hardware
gates intact and its failure policy stated.
</objective>

## Decisions

- **D-05 (fork F5): the lease covers `EpromOperator` only; `HardwareManager` keeps its own per-call connects.** Reason: one class, one seam, one revert. `HardwareManager` is a separate class with six of its own `find_and_connect`/`disconnect` sites, and the voltage sampler alone costs four connects per write step. Leaving it out is smaller and honest — and it caps the measurable saving. **The cap is stated here so the measurement is read correctly:** at three cycles the sampler's twelve connects survive, so roughly a third of a plan's connects are outside this lease and the measured saving should be expected to land below the theoretical connect-term total. If the measurement lands near the threshold, extending to `HardwareManager` is the named follow-up, and the cheaper sub-step already exists in the seed — folding `sample_vpp_mv` and `sample_vpe_mv` into one monitor read removes two connects per write step. Rating: `reversible`.
- **D-06 (fork F6): a `SerialError` inside a leased operation drops the lease; the next operation cold-connects.** Reason: the alternative — failing the plan — would make the lease *weaken* `run_plan`'s explicit invariant that one step's BAD verdict or raised exception never aborts the rest. A performance optimisation that changes failure semantics is a fidelity regression DEVTEST-03 would have to name, and that is not a trade worth making for a connect-time saving. Rating: `reversible`.
- **D-07-shape (fork F7, structural half): the lease ships as exactly two commits — A, the `setup_command` extraction, permanent; B, the lease itself, default-off, one call site, revertible whole.** Reason: SESS-02 requires the change be reverted rather than kept on principle if it does not pay, and a revert that is an unpick is a revert nobody performs. The threshold itself is pre-registered in plan 04, before the bench task, because a threshold chosen after the number is not a threshold. Rating: `reversible` — deliberately so, and this is the decision that makes it so.
- **D-11: the lease is acquired by `dev test` only. The STANDALONE `firestarter erase <chip> -b` command keeps its two port opens, and that is recorded as a scope boundary rather than omitted.** `205-CONTEXT.md` § Deferred Ideas named this phase as the home for "collapsing `erase -b`'s second port open into the erase's own session", and `205-SESSION-COST.md` measured that cost at 10.632 s on this rig. Two things are true and both belong in the record. **Inside `dev test` the cost is already collapsed by this lease:** the erasable recipe's cycle block is `[write, verify, erase, blank-check]`, so the erase step and the blank-check step are consecutive `EpromOperator` calls that share the leased link, and that saving is part of what SESS-02 measures. **The standalone CLI command is a different surface and is deliberately left alone:** SESS-01's requirement text is scoped to a `dev test` plan; a second call site would put a published-CLI behaviour change inside the one commit SESS-02 may require reverting, coupling a user-visible surface to a measurement outcome; and Phase 205 changed `erase -b`'s contract only one phase ago. Collapsing the standalone command is the named follow-up, and it becomes a one-line change the moment the lease survives SESS-02. Rating: `reversible`.
- **Deferred again: CMP-F1**, the `DONE`-based clean stop in `op_wait_for_ack`. A firmware change; the ROADMAP marks this phase `Repo: app`; no requirement of this phase names it.
